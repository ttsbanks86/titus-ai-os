# CFPB Complaint Template

**Use this template when filing a complaint with the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau.** The CFPB is the federal agency that oversees credit bureaus, debt collectors, banks, credit card companies, and other consumer financial institutions. They have authority to investigate and require response.

**When to use:**
- A credit bureau has failed to investigate a dispute properly
- A debt collector has violated the FDCPA
- A bank or creditor has mishandled fraud claims
- A credit reporting agency has refused to remove inaccurate information
- A consumer financial institution has engaged in unfair, deceptive, or abusive practices

**How to file:**
- Online: `https://www.consumerfinance.gov/complaint/`
- Phone: 1-855-411-2372
- Mail: Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, 1700 G Street NW, Washington, DC 20552 (write the complaint letter using this template and mail it in)

---

## What to Include in the Complaint

### Section 1: Your Information

- Full legal name
- Current address
- Phone number
- Email address
- Date of birth (if required)
- Last 4 of SSN (if required)

**Note:** The CFPB online portal collects some of this automatically. For mailed complaints, include it.

### Section 2: Company You Are Complaining About

- **Company name:** [Exact legal name of the company, e.g. "Equifax Information Services LLC"]
- **Company address:** [Headquarters or the address you have been corresponding with]
- **Company phone:** [Main customer service number]

### Section 3: Product and Issue

**Product category (pick one):**
- Credit reporting
- Debt collection
- Credit card
- Bank account or service
- Mortgage
- Student loan
- Money transfer
- Payday loan
- Other consumer financial product

**Sub-product (pick the most specific):**
- [e.g. "Credit reporting — General" / "Debt collection — Medical" / "Credit card — Other"]

**Issue (pick one or more):**
- Incorrect information on your report
- Unable to get information corrected
- Problem with fraud or identity theft response
- Communication tactics (FDCPA violation)
- Attempts to collect debt not owed
- Other

### Section 4: What Happened (Free-form Narrative)

This is the most important part. Write a clear, factual, chronological narrative. Be specific. Include dates, amounts, account numbers (last 4), names of representatives, and what you asked for vs what you got.

**Suggested structure (1-2 pages max):**

```
On [date], I discovered [problem] on my [credit report / account / etc.].

[1-2 sentences: what the problem is]

On [date], I [action you took: filed a dispute / contacted the company / etc.].

[1-2 sentences: what you asked for]

On [date], the company [response or non-response]. [Specifics:
"They responded on [date] that the item was 'verified' but provided no
documentation." / "They did not respond within 30 days as required by
FCRA 15 U.S.C. § 1681i." / "They continued collection activities despite
my cease and desist letter."]

I have [attempts to resolve: 1 written dispute, 2 phone calls, 1 email].
I am requesting that the CFPB [specific remedy: require the company to
remove the item, respond to my dispute, stop collection activities, etc.].

Supporting documentation is attached:
- Copy of my credit report showing the disputed item
- Copy of my dispute letter dated [date]
- Copy of the company's response dated [date]
- [Any other relevant documents]
```

### Section 5: What Resolution You Are Seeking

Common resolutions:
- Correct inaccurate information on credit report
- Remove an item from credit report
- Stop collection activities
- Respond to a dispute
- Refund or credit
- Damages (specify amount)
- Other

**Be specific.** The CFPB will not pursue vague complaints.

### Section 6: Supporting Documentation

Attach copies (not originals) of:
- Credit reports showing the disputed item
- Dispute letters and responses
- Account statements
- Call logs
- Collection notices
- Identity theft reports (if applicable)
- Any other relevant documents

## Sample Narrative (Generic — Customize to Your Situation)

```
I am writing to file a complaint against [Company Name] regarding their
handling of a credit report dispute.

On March 1, 2026, I requested a free copy of my credit report from
Equifax. The report included an account with [Creditor Name], account
ending in [XXXX], showing a balance of [$X,XXX] and a status of
"Charged-off." I did not open this account and I have no knowledge of it.

On March 5, 2026, I filed a dispute with Equifax via certified mail,
enclosing a copy of my government-issued ID and proof of my current
address. I requested that they investigate the account, verify it with
the original creditor, and remove it if it could not be verified.

On April 8, 2026, Equifax responded that the account had been "verified"
and would remain on my credit report. They provided no documentation of
the verification.

I filed a second dispute on April 20, 2026, with additional evidence.
Equifax again responded on May 15, 2026, that the account was "verified"
without providing documentation.

Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act, 15 U.S.C. § 1681i, Equifax is
required to conduct a reasonable investigation of disputes and to remove
information that cannot be verified. Their repeated refusal to provide
documentation of verification, and their continued reporting of an
account I did not open, is a violation of the FCRA.

I am requesting that the CFPB require Equifax to (1) conduct a proper
investigation of the disputed account, (2) provide me with documentation
of the verification, and (3) remove the account from my credit report
if it cannot be properly verified.
```

## What Happens After You File

1. **Acknowledgment (1-2 days):** CFPB sends you an email or letter confirming the complaint.
2. **Routing to the company (1-2 weeks):** CFPB forwards the complaint to the company. The company has 60 days to respond.
3. **Company response (within 60 days):** The company must respond to you and to the CFPB. They may resolve, partially resolve, or deny.
4. **Resolution or escalation (60+ days):** If the company resolves the issue, you can confirm in the CFPB portal. If not, you can dispute their response and the CFPB may investigate further.
5. **Public complaint database:** Your complaint (with personal info redacted) becomes part of the public Consumer Complaint Database. This is searchable by the public, journalists, and researchers. Companies take public complaints seriously.

---

## Tips

- **Be factual, not emotional.** Stick to dates, names, and outcomes.
- **Include the legal basis.** Cite the FCRA, FDCPA, EFTA, or other relevant law.
- **Be specific about the remedy.** "I want this fixed" is vague. "I want Equifax to remove the disputed account" is specific.
- **Keep copies.** Save the complaint and all responses.
- **Use the same portal for follow-up.** If the company does not respond in 60 days, return to the CFPB portal and indicate the company has not responded.
- **The CFPB is free.** Do not pay anyone to file a CFPB complaint for you. Scams exist.
