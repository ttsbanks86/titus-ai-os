# Debt Validation Request Template (FDCPA)

**Use this template within 30 days of first being contacted by a debt collector.** Under the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA), you have the right to demand that the collector prove they own the debt and the amount is correct. If they cannot, they must stop collection activities.

**This is the most powerful consumer tool against debt collectors. Use it FIRST before any pay-for-delete negotiation.**

---

## Letter Body (Send Within 30 Days of First Contact)

```
[Date]

[Collection Agency Name]
[Collection Agency Address]
[City, State, ZIP]

Re: Account [number, last 4]
    Original Creditor: [Original Creditor Name]
    Date of First Contact: [date you first heard from them]

To Whom It May Concern:

This letter is being sent in response to your recent communication regarding
the above-referenced account. Be advised this is NOT a refusal to pay, but
rather a formal notice that your claim is being disputed and validation is
hereby DEMANDED in accordance with the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act,
15 U.S.C. § 1692g, Section 809.

Within thirty (30) days, you are required to provide:

1. Verification, in writing, that this debt is valid and that you are
   licensed to collect on this debt in my state.
2. The name and address of the original creditor.
3. Documentation showing that I am legally obligated to pay this debt,
   including the original signed agreement giving rise to the debt.
4. The complete payment history, including all charges, fees, and
   interest added to the original balance.
5. A copy of the original account agreement with my signature.
6. Proof of the chain of assignment from the original creditor to
   your agency, if applicable.

Until you have complied with these requests, you must cease all collection
activities, including reporting this debt to any credit bureau.

Be advised that I am keeping careful records of all communications with
your office and your compliance with the FDCPA.

Sincerely,

[Your Signature]
[Your Printed Name]
[Your Address]
[Your Contact Information]
```

---

## What Happens After You Send

**The collector has 30 days to provide validation.** If they:

- **Provide valid validation:** The debt is real. Decide whether to pay, negotiate (pay-for-delete), or dispute further.
- **Cannot provide validation:** They must cease collection. The account should be removed from your credit reports. If it is not, dispute it with the bureaus.
- **Continue collection without validation:** This is a FDCPA violation. File a complaint with the CFPB (`https://www.consumerfinance.gov/complaint/`) AND the state Attorney General. You may also have a private right of action under FDCPA — consult an attorney.

---

## Critical Details

- **Send within 30 days of FIRST contact.** If the collector has been calling for 2 months already and you didn't send this, the FDCPA validation right may have lapsed. Send it anyway.
- **Send by certified mail.** Get proof of delivery. This is your evidence if you need to enforce.
- **Keep the original letter.** The 30-day clock starts when you send, not when they receive.
- **Don't acknowledge the debt.** Do not say "I owe this" or "I will pay." Just demand validation.
- **Don't make any payment.** Even a small payment can be construed as acknowledging the debt.
- **Don't give bank account information.** Even if they ask for "verification."

---

## When to Use

- Within 30 days of first contact from a debt collector
- Any debt you do not recognize or are not sure is yours
- Any debt you suspect has errors in the amount
- Old "zombie" debt that has resurfaced after years

## When NOT to Use

- You are certain the debt is yours and you want to pay it (just pay it)
- You want to negotiate pay-for-delete (do that AFTER validation succeeds, not instead of it)
- More than 30 days have passed since first contact (the FDCPA right may have lapsed, but you can still send a "cease and desist" letter or dispute the credit report)
