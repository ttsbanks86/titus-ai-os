# Pay-for-Delete Agreement Template

**Use this template to negotiate with a debt collector.** The deal: pay some or all of the debt in exchange for the collector removing the account from your credit reports. This is a legitimate negotiation tactic, but collectors are not legally required to agree.

**Important:** Get the pay-for-delete agreement IN WRITING before sending any payment. Verbal promises are worthless. The agreement should specify that the account will be removed from all 3 credit bureaus upon receipt of payment.

---

## Initial Letter (Request for Pay-for-Delete)

```
[Date]

[Collection Agency Name]
[Collection Agency Address]
[City, State, ZIP]

Re: Account [number, last 4]
    Original Creditor: [Original Creditor Name]
    Current Balance: [$amount]

To Whom It May Concern:

I am writing in regards to the above-referenced account, which appears on my
credit reports. I am interested in resolving this account in full.

I am willing to make a one-time payment of [$offer amount, typically 30-50%
of current balance] in exchange for the following:

1. Full deletion of this account from all three credit bureaus
   (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion).
2. A written confirmation that the account has been deleted from
   my credit reports, sent to me within 30 days of payment.
3. A written statement that the collection agency will not sell or
   transfer this account to a third party.

If you agree to these terms, please send me a written agreement on
company letterhead, signed by an authorized representative, before
I send any payment.

I look forward to your response.

Sincerely,

[Your Signature]
[Your Printed Name]
[Your Address]
[Your Contact Information]
```

---

## When the Collector Agrees (Confirm Before Paying)

The collector should send a written agreement. **Verify it includes:**

1. The exact account number
2. The exact payoff amount
3. The exact payment method (check, money order, ACH — avoid giving bank account info; certified funds are safest)
4. The exact deletion commitment ("will be deleted from all 3 credit bureaus within 30 days of payment")
5. The signature of an authorized representative
6. The date of the agreement
7. A "no resell" or "no transfer" clause

**If any of these are missing, push back before paying.**

---

## Payment Tips

- **Certified funds only.** Money order or cashier's check. Never wire money, never give bank account info, never pay with a personal check to a collector (they can drain your account).
- **Pay the agreed amount, no more.** If they try to demand more after the agreement, refuse and cite the agreement.
- **Get a receipt.** Ask for a "paid in full" receipt at the time of payment.
- **Document the date paid.** This starts the 30-day clock for the deletion confirmation.
- **Verify deletion.** Pull all 3 credit reports 30-45 days after payment. If the account is not deleted, dispute it as "paid but not deleted per agreement."

---

## When to Use

- Collection accounts on the credit report
- The user has some money available to negotiate
- The user is willing to pay something to get the account off the report
- The collection is still within the statute of limitations for the original debt

## When NOT to Use

- The collection is past the statute of limitations (a payment can restart the clock in some states — "zombie debt")
- The user cannot afford any payment
- The user disputes the validity of the debt (use debt validation request first, not pay-for-delete)
- The collector has been harassing the user (use cease and desist first, then negotiate from a position of strength)

---

## Statute of Limitations Warning

**CRITICAL:** In some states, making a payment on a time-barred debt RESTARTS the statute of limitations, making the debt legally collectible again. This is called "reviving" a zombie debt.

**Before negotiating a pay-for-delete on an old debt, confirm with OpenCode or consult an attorney whether the debt is past the statute of limitations in your state.** If it is, do NOT make any payment, do NOT acknowledge the debt in writing, and consider a cease and desist letter instead.
