# Cease and Desist Letter to Debt Collector Template

**Use this template to send a formal cease and desist letter to a debt collector.** Under the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA), you have the right to demand that a debt collector stop contacting you about a debt. After receiving this letter (in writing), the collector may contact you only to:
1. Acknowledge receipt of the letter
2. Notify you of a specific legal action (lawsuit) being taken
3. Inform you that they are ending collection efforts

**This is a powerful tool. Use it wisely.** It does NOT erase the debt. It does NOT prevent the collector from filing a lawsuit before the statute of limitations expires. It does NOT remove the account from your credit report.

**When to use:**
- A collector is calling repeatedly, harassing, or contacting you at work
- A collector is calling third parties (family, friends, employer) about the debt
- A collector is using threatening or abusive language
- A collector is calling outside permitted hours (before 8am or after 9pm local time)
- You want to buy time to validate the debt or consult an attorney

**When NOT to use:**
- You want to negotiate pay-for-delete (sending C&D first may end communication channels)
- You want to settle the debt quickly (negotiate first)
- The collector has already filed a lawsuit (you need an attorney, not a C&D letter)
- You are uncertain whether the debt is yours (send a debt validation request FIRST, not a C&D)

## Letter Body

```
[Date]

[Collection Agency Name]
[Collection Agency Address]
[City, State, ZIP]

Re: Cease and Desist Notice — Account [last 4 of XXXX]
    Original Creditor: [Original Creditor Name]

To Whom It May Concern:

This letter is being sent in response to your repeated contacts with me
regarding the above-referenced debt. Be advised that I am exercising
my right under the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act, 15 U.S.C.
§ 1692c(c), Section 805(c), to demand that you cease all communications
with me regarding this debt.

Effective immediately, you must:

1. Cease all telephone calls to my home, work, and mobile numbers.
2. Cease all written communications to my home, work, and email.
3. Cease all communications with my family, friends, employer,
   neighbors, or any third party regarding this debt.
4. Cease all contact with me EXCEPT as specifically permitted by
   15 U.S.C. § 1692c(c), which is limited to:
   a. Notifying me that you received this letter.
   b. Notifying me of a specific legal action being taken.
   c. Notifying me that you are terminating collection efforts.

You are further notified that:

1. I am aware of my rights under the FDCPA and other applicable laws.
2. I am keeping detailed records of all communications from your office.
3. Any violation of this cease and desist notice may subject your
   office to liability under 15 U.S.C. § 1692k, including statutory
   damages of up to $1,000 per violation, plus actual damages,
   attorneys' fees, and costs.

If you have legitimate legal action pending, please direct all future
communications to my attorney at the following address:

[Attorney Name, if applicable]
[Attorney Address]
[Attorney Phone]
[Attorney Email]

If you do not have legal action pending, you must cease all contact
with me.

I am not refusing to pay or acknowledge this debt by sending this
letter. This letter is solely a demand that you stop contacting me.

Sincerely,

[Your Signature]
[Your Printed Name]
[Your Address]
[Your Phone]
[Your Email]
```

## Important Details

- **Send by certified mail with return receipt.** Get proof of delivery. The clock starts when the letter is received.
- **Keep the original letter.** Save it with the certified mail receipt.
- **Note the date of receipt.** This is the date the collector was put on notice.
- **Do not give personal information beyond what is required.** The letter can be brief.
- **Do not threaten or get emotional.** Be factual and cite the law.
- **Update your contact info with credit bureaus** if you are not already monitoring. The collector may continue trying to reach you at the numbers they have on file.

---

## What Happens After You Send

**The collector must stop contacting you.** If they:
- **Comply:** The phone calls stop. The letters stop. You may receive one final letter acknowledging receipt. The debt may still appear on your credit report. The collector may still file a lawsuit before the statute of limitations.
- **Continue contacting you:** This is a FDCPA violation. You have a private right of action. Consult a consumer protection attorney. Many offer free consultations and work on contingency. Statutory damages up to $1,000 per violation plus actual damages and attorneys' fees.
- **File a lawsuit:** This is the only way they can continue trying to collect. You will receive a summons. **You MUST respond to the lawsuit within the deadline or you will lose by default judgment.** If you receive a lawsuit, consult an attorney immediately.

## Variations

### Variation 1: Cease and Desist with Debt Validation Request

If you want to BOTH validate the debt AND stop contact, send TWO letters:

1. **First letter:** Debt validation request (`debt-validation-request.md`) — requests proof of the debt
2. **Second letter (after validation response or 30 days):** This C&D letter — stops contact

Some people combine the two in one letter. This is fine. The key is that both requests are made.

### Variation 2: Cease and Desist with Payment in Full

If you want to settle the debt AND stop contact:

1. **Initial contact:** State that you intend to pay in full
2. **Pay the full amount** via certified funds
3. **Send a "paid in full" letter** confirming the payment
4. **Send a C&D letter** if continued contact occurs

### Variation 3: Cease and Desist Without an Attorney (Default)

If you do not have an attorney, omit the "future communications to my attorney" paragraph. The collector is still required to cease contact. The paragraph simply gives them a routing instruction if they choose to take legal action.

---

## After the C&D: What to Do With the Debt

Sending a C&D does not erase the debt. The collector may:
- Sell the debt to another collector (who will then start contacting you)
- File a lawsuit before the statute of limitations
- Continue reporting the account on your credit report

You still have options:
1. **Pay the debt** (if it's valid and you can afford to)
2. **Negotiate pay-for-delete** (only if you haven't C&D'd yet, since C&D cuts off communication)
3. **Dispute the credit report** (use `dispute-letter-general.md` to challenge the account)
4. **Wait for the statute of limitations to expire** (debt is then uncollectible through the courts)
5. **Consult a consumer protection attorney** (if the debt is significant, the C&D was violated, or a lawsuit is filed)
