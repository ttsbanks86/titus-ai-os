## Question 1

**We all make basic assumptions about life change. Which of the following in church is the MOST basic assumption we make?**

- A. Life change begins when you leave old friends behind for new ones.
- **B. Life change happens instantly at salvation.** ✅
- C. Life change is a process.

**Page Reference:** 68
**Quote:** *"One of the most basic assumptions made about life change is that it happens instantly at salvation."*
**Context:** This is the first of four common assumptions listed under "Four Assumptions Commonly Made About Life Change." The other three are: (2) it continues naturally over time, (3) it is achieved largely by an act of the will, and (4) it is best accomplished alone.

## Question 2

**What is an example of a right question the church should be asking?**

- A. What is the background of those present?
- B. How many people are present?
- **C. What are these people like?** ✅

**Page Reference:** 67
**Quote:** *"As Bill Hull has written, the wrong question for the church is How many people are present? The right question is What are these people like?"*
**Context:** This comes in the chapter "Rethinking Discipleship," where the focus shifts from counting attendance to assessing whether people are actually being transformed into the likeness of Christ.

## Question 3

**If you were communicating the ONLY barrier to discipleship in the church, what would it be?**

- A. Time
- B. Money
- **C. Effort** ✅

**Page Reference:** 69
**Quote:** *"the only barrier is their level of effort"*
**Context:** This is presented within the third common assumption: "Life Change Is Achieved Largely by an Act of the Will." While this assumption is later challenged by the book (which teaches that true change is about *training, not trying*), it reflects what is commonly communicated as the barrier to discipleship.

## Question 4

**When does optimal growth occur in the discipleship process?**

- A. When we commit ourselves to reading the Bible and praying a minimum of one hour per day.
- **B. When we develop a relationship with someone who knows something we don't or has experience in an area we don't and is willing to share this knowledge with us.** ✅
- C. When we determine we want Jesus more than we want the things of the world that are passing away daily.

**Page Reference:** 80-81
**Quote:** *"various types of small groups—and particularly the mentoring relationships they generate—'provide the optimal environment for incubating the maturing process'"*
**Context:** This appears under "Opportunities for Relationships," where the book argues that life change happens best through community, trust, and mentoring relationships where biblical truth is applied to real-life situations over extended periods.
