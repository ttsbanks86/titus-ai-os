# Brand Voice Cheatsheet

**Owner:** CDO (exec-cdo) + CMO (exec-cmo)
**Status:** Phase 0 Quick Win (QW5) — DRAFT v0.1
**Last updated:** 2026-06-06

This is the one-page voice guide every writer, designer, AI prompt, and content piece must follow. It is intentionally short. If it's not on this page, it doesn't go in the work.

---

## Voice at a Glance

**Titus Banks is a faith-rooted, family-first, practical, no-fluff voice. We speak like a wise older brother who has actually lived the thing — not a corporate brand, not a motivational speaker, not a preacher.**

### The 5 Anchors

1. **Clear** — say it once, say it plain. No jargon. No "in today's fast-paced world."
2. **Direct** — short sentences. Strong verbs. No hedging.
3. **Warm** — kind tone, not corporate-warm. We talk to people, not at them.
4. **Practical** — every insight should leave the reader knowing what to do.
5. **Grounded** — anchored in real life, family, faith, work. Not abstract.

---

## Do

- Open with the reader's reality, not a thesis statement.
- Use "you" and "we" generously. Almost never "users," "audiences," "consumers."
- Use concrete nouns (the kitchen table, the spreadsheet, Sunday morning) over abstract ones.
- End with a clear next step, a question, or a quiet line — not a slogan.
- Use scripture sparingly and only when it earns its place.
- Use plain contractions (don't, can't, it's).
- Use one strong image over three weak ones.

## Don't

- **No em dashes.** Use periods, commas, or parentheses instead.
- **No banned words:** "Elevate," "Seamless," "Unleash," "Next-Gen." Find a real word.
- **No hype:** "game-changing," "revolutionary," "transform your life" — banned by default.
- **No corporate filler:** "leverage," "synergy," "in today's world," "navigate the complexities of."
- **No emojis in UI** (no buttons, no nav, no headers). Emojis in social copy are okay only when human-feeling, max 1-2 per post, never in a row.
- **No all-caps in body copy.** Use them only in short overlines and labels.
- **No "we" when meaning "I."** Titus speaks for himself in first person when it's his story; the brand speaks as "we" when it's a community of readers.
- **No preaching at people.** Share from experience, let the reader decide.
- **No fake urgency.** No "limited time," "don't miss out," countdown timers (except genuine book launch countdown).
- **No sexualized, polarizing, or faith-mocking content.** CDO Faith/Values Gate applies.

---

## Sentence-Level Rules

- Average sentence length: 12-18 words.
- Vary rhythm. Short sentence. Then a longer one that explains it.
- Use periods more than commas. Break long thoughts into two sentences.
- Active voice by default. "I built the deck," not "The deck was built."
- One idea per sentence.

## Voice vs. Tone

**Voice is constant** (the 5 anchors above).
**Tone shifts by context** (allowed, documented in the table):

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---------|------|---------|
| Devotional / faith | Warm, slow, scripture-anchored | "Some mornings the coffee gets cold before the quiet does. That's okay." |
| LinkedIn BA / PM post | Direct, professional, useful | "Three requirements-gathering questions I ask on every project. The first one is the most skipped." |
| Book launch ad | Warm, story-led, clear CTA | "You were not built to break. You were built to be remade. The new book is here." |
| Email subject line | Curious, low-pressure | "A small thing I learned this week" |
| Error / 404 page | Human, lightly funny | "Even this page got struck down. Try the menu." |
| AI / tech explainer | Clear, no jargon, no hype | "A model is a pattern-spotter. It does not know you, and that's the point." |

## Quick Self-Check (run before publishing)

1. Could a pastor say this? (faith content)
2. Could a project manager say this? (work content)
3. Is there a banned word?
4. Is there an em dash?
5. Did I use corporate filler?
6. Is there a clear next step or a quiet landing?
7. Would I send this to my closest friend without cringing?

If 5 of 7 are "yes," ship it. If less, revise.
