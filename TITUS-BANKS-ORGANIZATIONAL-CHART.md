## How the 41 agents relate to each other

The CEO talks to the 7 executives directly. The CEO does NOT talk to the 30 sub-agents directly. The executives talk to their sub-agents. The sub-agents talk to each other within their department. Cross-department communication goes through the executive level.

**Exception:** The CEO can talk to any agent directly in an emergency. But emergencies should be rare. If the CEO is talking to sub-agents regularly, the executive layer is not working.

## What to build first (Week 1-2 priority)

| Priority | Agent | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | CFO | Financial clarity is the foundation. Know your numbers. |
| 2 | CTO | Infrastructure must be stable before scaling. |
| 3 | Content Director | Content is the growth engine. 3 posts per week. |
| 4 | CMO | Marketing turns content into leads. |
| 5 | Product Manager | Products are the revenue engine. |
| 6 | COO | Operations automates the repeatable. |
| 7 | Faith and Mission | Mission alignment prevents drift. |

**The order is: Money → Tech → Content → Marketing → Products → Operations → Mission.**

The reason: you cannot build content without a stable tech stack. You cannot market without content. You cannot sell products without marketing. You cannot automate without operations. You cannot maintain mission without all of the above being stable enough to reflect on.

## The single rule of the org chart

**The org chart is a map of functions, not a hierarchy of people.**

Every function must be filled by someone (human or agent). The same person can fill multiple functions in a one-person company. The org chart does not change when you hire. It changes when the business model changes.

The discipline is in the function, not the title. The discipline of tracking costs is the CFO function, regardless of whether it is done by Titus, by a sub-agent, or by a future hire. The discipline of the function is permanent. The agent behind it is replaceable.
