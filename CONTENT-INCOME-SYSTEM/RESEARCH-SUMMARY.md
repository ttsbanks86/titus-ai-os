# Research Summary

## Bottom Line

The profitable path is not fully automatic mass posting. The safer and more realistic path is a semi-automated content machine:

- Generate 100 post/video ideas at a time.
- Turn them into scripts, captions, and visuals in batches.
- Review the top 20 to 40 assets first.
- Schedule consistently through Brand2Social.
- Use comments and DMs to capture leads through approved automation.
- Monetize with services, affiliates, and digital products before relying on ad revenue.

## Tools For 100 Posts At A Time

### Canva Bulk Create

Best use: 100 static posts, quote cards, tip cards, carousel slides, checklist graphics, lead magnet promos.

Research notes:

- Canva Bulk Create can generate hundreds of design variations from rows and columns.
- It supports CSV/XLSX and Canva Sheets.
- Canva states Bulk Create can select up to 300 rows and 150 columns.
- Canva warns image URLs are not supported in some Bulk Create workflows; images must be uploaded or embedded.

Why it fits us:

- Great for clean, branded assets that do not look like random AI slop.
- Works well with our CSV/database approach.
- Best for Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, and Pinterest-style assets.

Limitation:

- It does not automatically create strong ideas. OpenCode/Marky should create the content table first.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/bulk-create/

### Marky

Best use: brand voice, topic generation, first drafts, caption direction, content calendar ideas.

Why it fits us:

- The workspace `The Open Door AI Systems` already exists.
- It already produced a fitting sample around `Prompting for Clarity`.
- Good for social post drafts and brand-consistent ideas.

Limitation:

- It should not be trusted to publish automatically until we inspect quality.
- It is not the strongest tool for bulk video creation.

### Predis.ai

Best use: AI social content generation, scheduling, bulk scheduling, multi-platform posts, ecommerce-style posts.

Research notes:

- Predis supports Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, YouTube Shorts, LinkedIn, Pinterest, Google Business, and X.
- It claims bulk scheduling for posts, carousels, stories, and videos.
- Published plan limits from its scheduler page: Core up to 65 posts/month, Rise up to 160 posts/month, Enterprise+ up to 500 posts/month.

Why it may fit later:

- It could replace or supplement Brand2Social if Brand2Social becomes limiting.
- It has official integrations and supports multiple accounts.

Limitation:

- Plan limits matter if we want 100+ posts per month.
- We already have Brand2Social, so this is not first priority unless Brand2Social cannot support the workflow.

Source: https://predis.ai/features/social-media-scheduler/

## Tools For 100 Videos At A Time

### InVideo AI

Best use: faceless educational videos, Shorts, Reels, script-to-video, stock footage videos, voiceover videos.

Research notes:

- InVideo says its faceless video maker writes scripts, adds media, voiceover, music, and sound effects.
- It uses stock libraries and prompt workflows.
- Pricing page content observed on the faceless page referenced annual plans starting around Plus at $17/mo billed annually, Max at $83/mo, Generative at $167/mo, with credits and storage limits.

Why it fits us:

- Strong candidate for videos that look more polished than raw AI slideshow content.
- Good for educational AI workflow videos where we need narration, screenshots, stock clips, and captions.

Limitation:

- Fully generated videos still need quality control. Bad scripts or generic stock footage can look low quality.
- Credits can become the bottleneck for 100 videos.

Source: https://invideo.io/make/ai-faceless-video-generator/

### Hypernatural

Best use: stylized AI videos, consistent characters, script-to-video, custom styles, short-form content.

Research notes:

- Hypernatural turns ideas/scripts into AI-generated videos.
- It supports custom styles, consistent characters, AI narration, captions, AI B-roll, and script-to-video.

Why it fits us:

- Better fit than generic stock-only videos if we want a unique visual identity.
- Potentially useful for recurring characters or consistent branded explainer style.

Limitation:

- Needs testing for output quality and export limits.
- Can still look obviously AI if the style is not chosen carefully.

Source: https://hypernatural.ai/ai

### CapCut / Canva Video

Best use: final cleanup, captions, pacing, brand kit, templates, resize, exports.

Why it fits us:

- AI tools generate rough cuts. CapCut/Canva make them look less cheap.
- Captions, pacing, hooks, and clean visual hierarchy matter more than just generation volume.

Limitation:

- More manual than fully automatic, but this is where quality improves.

### NVIDIA

Research finding:

- NVIDIA is important infrastructure behind AI video, avatars, GPUs, and generative AI tooling, but it is not the most practical direct creator tool for this project.
- For our 7-day goal, use creator-facing tools like InVideo, Hypernatural, Canva, Marky, Brand2Social, and CapCut.

Recommendation:

- Do not build the first version around NVIDIA unless a specific NVIDIA-powered product gives a clear workflow advantage.

## Monetization Evidence

### Creator money is real, but uneven

Goldman Sachs estimated the creator economy could reach about $480B by 2027 and noted creators earn primarily through brand deals, platform ad share, subscriptions, donations, and direct payments. It also stated brand deals are the main source of revenue at about 70% in survey data, while only about 4% of global creators are professionals earning over $100,000 per year.

Source: https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/the-creator-economy-could-approach-half-a-trillion-dollars-by-2027

### Brands are spending, but they want trust and fit

IAB reported creator economy ad spend projected to reach $37B in 2025, with creators becoming a must-buy channel for many advertisers. The same report said brands care about creator reputation and audience alignment, and 95% of advertisers have concerns about AI in creator marketing, especially loss of human connection.

Source: https://www.iab.com/news/creator-economy-ad-spend-to-reach-37-billion-in-2025-growing-4x-faster-than-total-media-industry-according-to-iab/

### Practical implication

The system must build trust. If the pages look automated, generic, fake, or repetitive, monetization chances drop.

## Platform Policy Risks

### YouTube

YouTube states monetized content should be original and authentic. It specifically warns that mass-produced, repetitive, template-driven content with little variation can be ineligible for monetization. It also warns about reused content without significant commentary, transformation, or educational value.

Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en

Implication:

- Do not upload 100 nearly identical AI videos.
- Each video needs a unique practical lesson, story, workflow, example, or demonstration.

### Meta Messenger and Instagram Messaging

Meta states businesses have up to 24 hours to respond to a user. Messages inside that window may contain promotional content. It also requires automated bots to respond within 30 seconds to user input and recommends disclosure when users are interacting with automation.

Source: https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/messenger-platform/policy

Implication:

- Use official Brand2Social/Meta integrations only.
- Automate responses only to user-initiated comments/messages.
- Include clear automation disclosure where appropriate.
- Do not cold-DM people.

## Recommendation

The first version should be:

- One core brand.
- Five platform presences max.
- 100 post ideas generated locally.
- 30 to 50 approved posts scheduled first.
- 20 to 30 videos generated first, not 100 immediately.
- One lead magnet CTA.
- One service offer.
- Affiliate links only after trust and proper disclosure are in place.

This gives scale without triggering the low-quality automation trap.
